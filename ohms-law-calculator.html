The relationship behind V = IR

Voltage, current, and resistance in a circuit are locked together by Ohm's Law. Knowing any two lets you solve for the third, which is why this tool only needs two of the three values to compute the missing one.

Where Ohm's Law doesn't apply cleanly

The law assumes a simple resistive (ohmic) component. Parts like diodes and transistors don't have constant resistance and don't follow a straight-line voltage-current relationship, so Ohm's Law alone won't accurately describe their behavior.

Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law is a foundational principle in electrical engineering and physics that states the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in an electrical circuit. Discovered by physicist Georg Ohm, the law establishes that the electrical current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it, and inversely proportional to the circuit's total resistance. This rule acts as the basic roadmap for designing, diagnosing, and repairing any electrical circuit, from simple flashlights to microscopic computer processors, ensuring components receive safe operational power levels.

Ohm's Law Calculator Components

The mathematical model links three distinct electronic metrics, allowing users to calculate any single trait by providing the other two.

  • Voltage (V): The electrical potential difference or driving pressure that pushes electric charges through a loop, measured in Volts.
  • Current (I): The physical flow rate of electric charge moving past a specific point in the circuit, measured in Amperes or Amps.
  • Resistance (R): The natural opposition or friction a material exerts against the passage of electrical current, measured in Ohms.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens to the current in a circuit if the resistance doubles?

If the resistance in a circuit doubles while the voltage source remains completely steady, the current flow drops exactly in half. Increasing resistance creates more friction, restriction, and drag against the movement of electrical charges.

What is the difference between direct current (DC) and alternating current (AC)?

Direct current forces electrical charge to flow continuously in one single direction, typical of batteries and electronics. Alternating current periodically reverses its flow direction at fixed intervals, which is the universal standard for grid power distribution systems.

How does Ohm's Law help prevent electrical fires?

If a circuit experiences zero resistance, known as a short circuit, the current instantly surges to dangerous levels. This massive current generation creates extreme heat inside the wiring, melting insulation layers and sparking fires unless safety fuses pop to break the line.

Can I apply Ohm's Law directly to alternating current (AC) appliances?

Ohm's Law applies directly to simple AC heating and lighting elements. However, complex AC appliances with motors or transformers introduce secondary magnetic restrictions known as impedance, which requires advanced vector formulas to track accurately.
