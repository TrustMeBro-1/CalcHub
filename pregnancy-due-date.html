Pregnancy & Due Date Tracker: Pregnancy Due Date Calculator - Estimate Your Delivery Date

Estimate a due date and current week of pregnancy based on the first day of the last menstrual period.

How the estimate is calculated

The standard method, Naegele's rule, adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period, assuming a typical 28-day cycle — which is why it's called an estimated due date, not a fixed one.

Why so few births land exactly on the date

Full-term birth is considered a range, roughly 37–42 weeks, and cycle length variation or irregular periods can shift the real date from the calculated one. An early ultrasound typically refines this estimate more accurately than LMP-based dating alone.

Pregnancy Due Dates

A pregnancy due date calculator estimates an expectant mother's projected delivery date by tracking biological markers and gestational milestones. Standard human pregnancies average roughly 280 days, or 40 weeks, starting from the very first day of the last menstrual period. Because exact conception windows are often difficult to isolate, tracking clinical cycle patterns offers a highly reliable method for establishing developmental timelines. Predicting this timeline helps families plan medical checkups, track fetal development markers, and organize essential lifestyle adjustments well ahead of arrival.

Due Date Calculator Components

Projecting clinical delivery windows requires inputting individual physiological metrics to map out your structural timeline.

  • Last Period Date: The calendar date matching the very first day of bleeding during your last normal menstrual cycle.
  • Average Cycle Length: The typical number of days passing between the start of one period and the start of the next, defaulting to 28 days.
  • Conception Date (Optional): The exact calendar day of fertilization, used if tracking via structured ovulation monitoring or medical fertility routines.

The Clinical Calculation Rules

The tracking utility applies standard obstetrical rules to translate your calendar inputs into a developmental timeline target.

  • Naegele's Rule: The standard clinical formula takes the first day of your last period, adds seven calendar days, subtracts three calendar months, and advances the calendar year by one.
  • Gestational Adjustments: The calculator shifts your timeline forward or backward if your personal menstrual cycle duration is consistently shorter or longer than the standard 28-day baseline.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How accurate is an estimated pregnancy due date?

An estimated due date serves as a generalized target window rather than a strict deadline promise. Only about 4 to 5 percent of babies are born precisely on their calculated due date, though the vast majority arrive safely within a two-week window before or after that estimated target.

What is the difference between gestational age and fetal age?

Gestational age measures pregnancy progress starting from the first day of your last period, which is the universal standard utilized by medical professionals. Fetal age measures the actual age of the growing baby from the precise moment of conception, making it roughly two weeks younger than the gestational age track.

Can an early ultrasound change my official calculated due date?

Yes. Early first-trimester ultrasounds measure physical crown-to-rump dimensions to establish exceptional developmental tracking accuracy. If those physical ultrasound measurements differ from your period-based calculation by more than seven days, your healthcare provider will update your official due date.

What does it mean if a baby is considered full term?

A pregnancy is clinically recognized as full term once it reaches 39 weeks of developmental gestation. Delivering during this window ensures the baby's vital organs, specifically the lungs and brain tissue, have reached full maturity, minimizing neonatal care complications.
