Why "words per page" isn't a fixed number

A page is not a fixed unit. Font, font size, line spacing, margins, paragraph length, headings, quotations, images, and headers/footers all change the result. This tool shows you a realistic range based on your exact format, not a false single number.

How this converter works

Transparent assignment planning: Students need quick progress targets, teachers need format-aware consistency, and parents need a clear way to help without guessing. All three benefit when the tool visibly explains that page count is only an estimate.

Format matters: This converter respects that a page in double-spaced 12pt Times New Roman with 1-inch margins looks different from the same 1,000 words in single-spaced 11pt Arial. Select your exact format and we'll give you a realistic range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do you show a range instead of one number?

Real documents contain headings, quotations, references, tables, and whitespace—things that take up space without adding proportional word count. A 3.4–3.9 page range is honest. A single "3.6" looks falsely precise.

Will my actual page count match this estimate?

The estimate is your guide, but the page count in your actual editor (Word, Google Docs, etc.) is the final authority. Copy your exact text into this tool, select your format, and use the range as your target. Then verify in your submission editor.

What if my document has lots of quotes, headings, or images?

Use the "Include in your document" toggles to account for title pages, block quotes, references, and other non-body content. These add pages without proportional word count. The estimate will adjust accordingly.

Can I use this for fiction, technical writing, or non-academic papers?

This converter is built for academic assignments. If you're writing fiction or a published manuscript, page count depends on professional typesetting standards, which differ significantly from school papers. For those, we recommend checking your target publisher's guidelines.

I need both a page range AND a word range. How do I set that up?

If your teacher said "4–5 double-spaced pages, roughly 1,000–1,250 words," use the Pages → Words mode. That answers "How many words should I aim for?" more directly than page-to-page conversion.
